## Don't multitask
There are absolutely times when multitasking is necessary, but try to minimize them as much as possible. *Especially* in your personal time. 
If your evening commute consists of recording voice notes while dodging E-bikes, with music is blasting in the background, you're probably not able to appreciate the setting sun.
If your Saturday morning routine consists of, watching AMTA/Minecraft parkour on your phone, while eating, with Parks and Rec playing in the background, you're probably barely registering notes of hazelnut the coffee you just brewed.
I don't mean to imply that everyone needs to be registering the notes of hazelnut in their coffee. I only mean that our senses our limited. When we focus on more than one thing, parts of each of those things are lost. When I'm lucky enough to be faced with something I really enjoy or value, I find it fulfilling to five it my full attention. 

Multitasking is part of the larger subject of focus. If we define a task as something more abstract than a physical activity, this idea of multitasking can apply to all sorts of mental load. And I think this is something very intuitive to us, if there's an important email you're waiting to hear back on, or if there's an important conversation we're anticipating in the near future, it can be hard to give any one thing our full attention. These sorts of thoughts are often called distractions, but distraction sort of implies there's something that you *ought* to be focusing on, so I'm not sure how I like the word in the context of leisure.
